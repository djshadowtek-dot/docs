Google Infrastructure holds an extensive portfolio of third-party certifications and regulatory authorizations to help you meet your own compliance obligations. Rather than proving compliance from scratch, you inherit Google's controls as a baseline and layer your own controls on top. This shared responsibility model means you can spend less time auditing infrastructure fundamentals and more time building compliant applications. This guide covers the certifications available, how to access audit documentation, and which platform tools help you enforce compliance requirements programmatically.

## Compliance Certifications

Google Infrastructure maintains compliance with the following frameworks and regulations. Each certification is validated by independent third-party auditors and renewed on a regular cadence.

| Framework | Scope |
|---|---|
| **SOC 1 / SOC 2 / SOC 3** | Controls relevant to financial reporting, security, availability, and confidentiality. SOC 3 reports are publicly available; SOC 1 and SOC 2 reports are available under NDA. |
| **ISO 27001** | Information security management system (ISMS) covering risk management, asset control, and access management. |
| **ISO 27017** | Cloud-specific security controls extending ISO 27001 for cloud service providers and customers. |
| **ISO 27018** | Protection of personally identifiable information (PII) in public cloud environments. |
| **PCI DSS Level 1** | The highest tier of Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard compliance, covering services used to process, store, or transmit cardholder data. |
| **HIPAA** | Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act compliance for services covered under a Business Associate Agreement (BAA). |
| **FedRAMP High** | Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program authorization for U.S. government workloads requiring the highest security baseline. |
| **FedRAMP Moderate** | FedRAMP authorization covering the majority of unclassified federal information systems. |
| **GDPR** | Technical and organizational controls supporting compliance with the EU General Data Protection Regulation, including data processing agreements and data subject rights tooling. |

<Info>
  Google Infrastructure's Business Associate Agreement (BAA) for HIPAA is available to all customers at no additional charge. The BAA covers a defined set of HIPAA-eligible services. Before processing protected health information (PHI) on Google Infrastructure, sign the BAA in the Cloud Console under **Account → Settings → Legal** and verify that each service you plan to use is listed as a covered service.
</Info>

## Accessing Compliance Reports

The **Compliance Reports Manager** in the Cloud Console gives you on-demand access to Google's audit reports, certifications, and compliance documentation — including SOC reports, ISO certificates, and PCI AOC letters — without requiring you to go through a Google account team.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Compliance Reports Manager">
    Navigate to **IAM & Admin → Compliance Reports Manager** in the Cloud Console, or visit [console.cloud.google.com/iam-admin/compliance](https://console.cloud.google.com/iam-admin/compliance).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a report">
    Browse the list of available reports. Filter by certification type, applicable service, or time period to find the specific documentation you need for an audit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Accept the terms and download">
    Some reports — such as SOC 2 Type II — require acceptance of a non-disclosure agreement before download. Accept the terms and download the report as a PDF. You can share the downloaded report directly with your auditors.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Enforcing Controls with Organization Policy Service

The **Organization Policy Service** lets you set constraints across your entire resource hierarchy — preventing actions that could violate your compliance posture, regardless of what individual IAM permissions allow. For example, you can restrict which regions resources can be created in, require that all VMs use Shielded VM configuration, or disable service account key creation organization-wide.

Define your policy in a JSON file and apply it using the CLI:

```bash
gcloud org-policies set-policy policy.json
```

An example policy that restricts resource creation to specific regions:

```json
{
  "name": "projects/my-project/policies/gcp.resourceLocations",
  "spec": {
    "rules": [
      {
        "values": {
          "allowedValues": [
            "in:us-locations",
            "in:eu-locations"
          ]
        }
      }
    ]
  }
}
```

List available constraints to find the right policy for your compliance requirement:

```bash
gcloud org-policies list-available-constraints --organization=ORG_ID
```

## VPC Service Controls

**VPC Service Controls** lets you define security perimeters around your Google Cloud APIs and services. Resources inside a perimeter can communicate with each other, but requests originating from outside the perimeter — including from the public internet or from other Google Cloud projects — are denied by default. This prevents data exfiltration even in scenarios where IAM permissions have been misconfigured or compromised.

```bash
gcloud access-context-manager perimeters create my-perimeter \
  --title="Sensitive Data Perimeter" \
  --resources=projects/PROJECT_NUMBER \
  --restricted-services=storage.googleapis.com,bigquery.googleapis.com \
  --policy=POLICY_ID
```

Use dry-run mode to evaluate the impact of a perimeter before enforcing it, minimizing the risk of disrupting legitimate access patterns.
